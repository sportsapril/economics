## 🏛️ What's New From the Fed

**Governor Christopher Waller signaled openness to a September hold (Sept. 3).** In a speech titled "The Economic Outlook and Some Comments on My Policy Communication" at the Reuters NEXT Newsmaker Interview, Waller said inflation remains "meaningfully above" the FOMC's 2% goal but that recent data show "signs of disinflation." ## 🎙️ Sentiment Read

The Committee looks genuinely split heading into blackout, and the last 24 hours widened that split rather than resolving it. Chair Kevin Warsh — in office since May 22, 2026, succeeding Jerome Powell — struck a hawkish tone at Jackson Hole and in his first FOMC meeting (July 29) presided over a shorter-than-normal statement and a 9–3 vote with three hawkish dissents from regional presidents arguing inflation "above the Fed's 2% target for more than five years" warranted a hike. That pushed hike odds to ~66%.

Waller's Sept. 3 remarks pulled the other way, explicitly conditioning a hold on "continued progress" in the data due before the meeting (notably the August CPI report, released ~Sept. 11, and today's jobs report). His language ("I am willing to support holding") is a real policy signal from a sitting governor, not just a data observation — and it moved market pricing about 12–16 percentage points in a matter of minutes, per CME FedWatch tracking. That sensitivity itself signals a Committee where the swing votes are genuinely undecided, making the next two data releases before the meeting more consequential than usual.
