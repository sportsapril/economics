## 🎙️ Sentiment Read

The Committee is visibly split, and the split is anchored in specific, quotable language rather than vague tone:

- **Chair Kevin Warsh (hawkish)** — Aug 28 Jackson Hole speech "In Our Time": *"While this summer's [inflation] readings were better than expected, they do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved."* He also criticized the Fed's use of forward guidance ("You can call it an outline, you can call it a trail map, just don't call it forward guidance… it has overstayed its welcome"), signaling less commitment to a pre-set path and more meeting-by-meeting flexibility to raise rates if needed.

- **Governor Christopher Waller (dovish pushback)** — Sept 3 Reuters Newsmaker interview: *"Give disinflation a chance. We can wait one meeting."* He said he'd support holding rates steady if the (then-upcoming, now imminent) August CPI report showed continued cooling, but would "consider a rate hike" if inflation came in hot.

- **Governor Michael Barr** has separately indicated he would back a hike if inflation doesn't moderate soon enough (reported, not a direct quote reviewed in this brief).

Net effect: the Committee's own public communication has been the single biggest swing factor in market pricing over the past two weeks — more than any single data point — with hike odds oscillating between ~30% and ~71% as different officials spoke. That instability is itself a signal that the September decision is genuinely live, not a foregone conclusion in either direction.

## 📊 Implications for the Broad Stock Market

- **Rate-hike risk is repricing equities lower.** The S&P 500 fell for a fourth straight session on Sept 10 (-0.58% to 7,591.70), with the Dow (-0.6%) and Nasdaq (-0.65%) also down, as hike odds rose alongside oil prices. The VIX jumped 8.7% to 17.89, indicating rising hedging demand. (Market data, not commentary.)

- **Energy-driven inflation complicates the growth-vs-value and sector picture.** A hike driven by an oil-supply shock (Iran conflict, WTI >$100, Brent >$107) rather than domestic demand strength is a different animal than a demand-driven hike: it raises the risk of stagflationary pressure (weaker growth + sticky prices), which historically has been unfriendly to both long-duration growth/tech stocks (higher discount rates) and cyclicals exposed to input costs. Energy stocks are a likely relative beneficiary of the oil move itself.

- **Bond-yield reaction has been sharp.** The 10-year yield touching its highest level since 2023 reflects markets pricing both a higher near-term policy rate and, potentially, term-premium concerns about inflation persistence. Higher long yields typically pressure equity valuations broadly, with the most rate-sensitive sectors (real estate, utilities, small caps reliant on floating-rate debt) most exposed.

- **Cited analyst commentary (labeled as commentary):** Reuters and CNBC reporting frames the Sept 11 CPI print as "even more important than usual" for determining whether the Fed hikes or holds on Sept 16 — outlets characterize the September decision as a genuine "coin flip" given the speed with which officials' public remarks have swung market pricing. - **Divergence between core CPI (2.5% y/y) and core PCE (3.3% y/y)** is unusually wide by historical standards; if this gap persists into the August prints, it may itself become a market talking point about which gauge the Fed leans on, since the two measures are giving notably different reads on how close inflation is to target.
