## 🎙️ Sentiment Read

This cycle is unusual: the debate isn't cut-vs-hold, it's **hold-vs-hike**. Inflation has run persistently above the Fed's 2% target — Gov. Cook said in her Aug 5 speech that it has stayed above target "for more than five years" — aggravated recently by an oil-price shock tied to the ongoing U.S.–Iran conflict and its effect on the Strait of Hormuz, plus tariffs and immigration-related labor-supply tightening. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has "signaled he's determined to drive inflation back down to the central bank's 2% annual target," per CBS News, and going into Friday, futures were "decidedly tilted toward the Fed hiking its benchmark rate in September."

Friday's weak jobs print flipped the market's near-term lean from "hike" toward "hold," but it did not create a dovish consensus. Some economists still expect the Fed to prioritize inflation over the labor market: **Bank of America economists said Friday, "We agree that the July jobs report was a bit dovish on net. But we are sticking with our call that the Fed will hike by [0.75 percentage points] this year, starting in September. The Fed is likely to remain more focused on inflation than labor."** By contrast, Indeed Hiring Lab's Cory Stahle said the report might push the Fed to "think about the timing of a potential rate hike, if not think about some rate cuts on the table as well, if we continue to see this type of deterioration in the labor market." No FOMC voting member issued fresh public commentary on this specific tension during the window (Bowman's Saturday chat had no available transcript). [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/federal-reserve-september-rate-decision-jobs-report-kevin-warsh/)
