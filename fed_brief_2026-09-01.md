## 🎙️ Sentiment Read
Hawkish, and more hawkish than the Fed's own July meeting-era communication. Warsh's Jackson Hole framing was more pointed than his post-July-FOMC tone: he explicitly declined to treat the July CPI/PCE softening as evidence that underlying inflation trends have improved, and he leaned on breadth statistics (54% of PCE components above 3% annualized over 12 months, 49% over 6 months) rather than the headline print alone — a rhetorical move that signals he wants more evidence before easing, and potentially wants to tighten. Market pricing moved in lockstep: odds of a September hike roughly doubled (from ~35% to ~65%) in the days since the speech. That is the sharpest documented shift in this reporting window, even though the FOMC target range itself hasn't moved and no new statement has been issued. The weekend's U.S.-Iran escalation is a separate, non-Fed catalyst that reinforced the same rate-hike-friendly direction by pushing oil and long yields higher, but it is a geopolitical/energy shock, not a monetary-policy signal, and should be read as compounding rather than confirming the Fed's own hawkish tilt.
