## 🎙️ Sentiment Read

The dominant fact shaping sentiment isn't new today, but it's still the active story: at the July 29 meeting, the FOMC held rates steady 9–3, and for the first time since September 2016, three policymakers (Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari, Dallas's Lorie Logan) dissented with a unified hawkish view — preferring to *raise* the target range by 25bp rather than hold. The statement itself leaned hawkish: "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain sectors, including energy. The Committee will deliver price stability." New Chair Kevin Warsh (confirmed by the Senate 54–45 on May 13, seated May 22) followed with prepared remarks read by several outlets as hawkish even though markets initially reacted as if he'd been dovish; JPMorgan pulled forward its rate-hike call to December within hours of the press conference. Since then, Kashkari has said explicitly that "now is the time to start slowly moving" rates up, and Hammack has said the weak July jobs report didn't change her hawkish outlook.

This week's two cooler-than-expected inflation prints (CPI Tuesday, PPI Thursday) have measurably softened that hawkish pricing — September hike odds nearly halved from 55% to 32% in three trading days — but commentators caution against reading it as an all-clear: gasoline prices have resumed climbing (Strait of Hormuz tensions with Iran are the proximate driver) even as this month's data reflected earlier, lower average crude prices. The Fed under Warsh is being described by market commentary as "genuinely data-dependent" ahead of the September 15–16 meeting, meaning the next CPI/PCE/jobs prints — not this week's speeches — are likely to be the swing factor. Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/31/kevin-warsh-fed-inflation-rate-hike-markets.html), [24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/13/inflation-falls-for-2-months-straight-is-a-fed-september-rate-hike-still-on-the-table/).
