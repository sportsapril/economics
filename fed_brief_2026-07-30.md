## 🏛️ What's New From the Fed

**FOMC held rates at a 9–3 vote with a rare hawkish triple-dissent (July 28–29 meeting).** The Committee maintained the federal funds target range at 3-1/2 to 3-3/4 percent. Three regional presidents — Beth M. Hammack, Neel Kashkari, and Lorie K. Logan — dissented in favor of a 25bp *hike*, an unusually hawkish dissent pattern (dissents are typically for cuts, not hikes). Key quote from the statement: "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain sectors, including energy. The Committee will deliver price stability." Source: [Federal Reserve issues FOMC statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260729a.htm).

**Statement format continues to shrink under Chair Kevin Warsh.** As in June, the July statement dropped standard forward-guidance boilerplate in favor of a short, "just the facts" format. Chair Warsh, in his post-meeting press conference, said the new format "just gives you the facts, as best we can judge it." Source: [CNBC — Fed decision redline](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/fed-decision-redline-heres-what-changed-in-the-second-statement-under-warsh.html).

**Warsh press conference: explicitly hawkish, no forward guidance on rate path.** Key quotes: "There is no soft inflation target, there is no soft implicit target — not on this Committee's watch. There is only a target, and it is 2 percent." On the divided vote: "I asked for a good family fight, and I got one. That's the purpose. That's the design feature." Warsh said the Fed "will not hesitate to act" where necessary and that higher rates "could well be part of the solution" to above-target inflation. He also said the Fed is deliberately not pushing back on the recent rise in long-end Treasury yields, treating it as a market signal rather than something to suppress. Sources: [CNBC live recap](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/fed-meeting-today-live-updates.html), [Fed press conference transcript (PDF)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/mediacenter/files/FOMCpresconf20260729.pdf).

No SEP/dot plot at this meeting (only released at March/June/Sept/Dec meetings). No Beige Book or additional speeches/testimony in the last 24 hours beyond the meeting itself — this was the dominant, and essentially only, Fed news of the period.

## 🎙️ Sentiment Read

**Hawkish, and more explicitly so than the June meeting.** Anchoring points:

1. **The dissent direction is the clearest signal.** Three voting members wanted to *hike* — not cut — a configuration rarely seen and one that pulls the "center of gravity" of the Committee's revealed preferences to the hawkish side of the 9-person majority that held.
2. **Warsh's direct rejection of any "soft" inflation target** ("There is no soft inflation target... There is only a target, and it is 2 percent") signals unwillingness to tolerate above-target inflation for an extended period, and explicitly floats hikes ("could well be part of the solution") rather than only holds.
3. **No effort to talk down the bond selloff.** Warsh's choice to let long-end yields rise rather than push back is itself a hawkish communication choice — a Fed actively resisting easing financial conditions is different from one passively tolerating them.
4. **Statement brevity continues** — the move away from forward guidance under Warsh (a pattern that started in June) removes the dovish-leaning language that had been in the statement as recently as April, and is being read by markets as deliberately keeping optionality open toward hikes.
