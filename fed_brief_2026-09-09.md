## 🎙️ Sentiment Read

The committee's center of gravity has shifted hawkish since midsummer, though the signal is genuinely split rather than one-directional. The July 28–29 minutes show the debate moved from "when to cut" to "whether to hike": three dissents favored raising rates immediately, and "many" participants said tightening would likely be needed if inflation didn't decline — a materially different tone than earlier in the year. Chair Warsh amplified that on Aug. 28 with a hawkish data framework at Jackson Hole that markets read as pre-positioning for a hike. Governor Waller pushed back on Sept. 3, explicitly asking colleagues to "give disinflation a chance" for one more meeting — but he hedged that view on incoming data, not on principle.

With the Fed now silenced by blackout, the marginal driver of sentiment this week has not been Fed rhetoric but the oil shock from the U.S.-Iran conflict in the Strait of Hormuz and a hot August jobs report — both of which push in the same (hawkish) direction by threatening to keep headline inflation elevated even as the Fed's preferred core gauge (core PCE) has been flat at 3.3% for two months. The August and July inflation prints (CPI due Sept. 11, ahead of the meeting) are now the single most consequential data point left before the decision.
