## 🎙️ Sentiment Read
The Fed's tone is genuinely split, and this is a rare period where the market-implied risk skews toward a **hike**, not a cut. The July 28–29 statement held rates steady, but three regional presidents (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan, per market-preview reporting) formally dissented in favor of tightening. The August 19 minutes reportedly showed that hawkish concern extended well beyond those three dissenters — several additional participants said inflation was "not declining fast enough" and that the current rate level "may be insufficient to return price stability within a reasonable timeframe," while other participants flagged labor-market softening. The committee agreed to stay data-dependent ahead of September.

Chair Warsh, who took office in May 2026, has notably stripped forward guidance from Fed communications and given deliberately vague post-meeting press conference answers — a communication-style shift that itself is being read as a change in regime, separate from where rates actually go. His Friday Jackson Hole keynote (theme: "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy") is explicitly framed by Warsh himself as addressing "big picture questions" rather than near-term guidance, but markets will parse it closely regardless, since it is the last scheduled Fed communication before the September 15–16 meeting and before the release of August CPI and jobs data.

Net read: modestly hawkish-leaning uncertainty. The weak July jobs report has pulled hike odds down from ~82% to ~32% over three weeks, but underlying inflation readings (CPI 3.4% y/y, core PCE 3.3% y/y) remain stuck well above target, keeping a September hike a live possibility rather than a tail risk.
