## 🎙️ Sentiment Read

The July 29 statement and vote are more hawkish-leaning than a standard "hold" headline suggests: all three dissents wanted to hike immediately, not cut — an unusual dissent pattern that signals real hawkish pressure inside the Committee. The Fed's own SEP puts year-end 2026 rate projections in a wide 3.6%–4.1% band, consistent with some officials still expecting a hike this year.

Market pricing this week reflects a genuine tug-of-war rather than a clear directional read: the weak July jobs report (-23k) pulled September hike odds down to a low of ~44%, but odds rebounded to ~52% within days as oil prices jumped roughly 5% on stalled Iran/Strait of Hormuz negotiations, reviving inflation concerns ahead of Wednesday's CPI print. Capital Economics' Jonas Goltermann said "we think the risks are skewed towards a hot [CPI] print, which would probably drive a rebound in rate expectations and, potentially, renewed worries about stagflation" (commentary, not fact). Separately, the renewed Trump-vs-Cook fight introduces a Fed-independence risk premium that several strategists (Barclays, SGH Macro's Tim Duy) argue the long end of the Treasury curve is not yet pricing correctly — that's a distinct, longer-horizon concern layered on top of the near-term hike/hold debate.
