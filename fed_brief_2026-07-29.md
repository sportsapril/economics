## 🎙️ Sentiment Read

Distinctly hawkish — unusually so for a modern FOMC, and a genuine departure from the rate-cut narrative that dominated coming into 2026. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh (confirmed in the closest Senate vote for a Fed chair in the modern era, 54–45, and sworn in May 22) used his first semiannual testimony to Congress (July 14–15) to tell lawmakers the FOMC has "no tolerance for persistently elevated inflation" and that "the inflation surge of the last five years will be a thing of the past" if the Fed gets policy right — language centered on inflation-fighting rather than the employment side of the dual mandate. Notably, even when a favorable June CPI reading landed the same day as his testimony, Warsh cautioned it was "just one month of data," resisting any "mission accomplished" framing. [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/kevin-warsh-fed-testimony/), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/15/nx-s1-5894712/in-first-congressional-testimony-new-fed-chief-warsh-vows-to-fight-inflation)

The committee's own June projections back this up: at the June 17 meeting, the median dot for year-end 2026 jumped to 3.8% from 3.4% in March — a flip from a projected cut to an implied hike — with 9 of 18 officials penciling in at least one increase and 17 of 18 seeing inflation risks tilted to the upside.

Market pricing has moved in step: hike odds for today's meeting near 38% (from ~11% two weeks ago) and September hike odds near 82% (from ~53% a week ago). The proximate driver is geopolitical, not domestic-demand inflation: the Iran/Strait of Hormuz standoff has re-escalated — President Trump's blockade move rattled markets on July 26 — pushing gasoline and energy costs, and headline CPI, back up after June's truce-driven cooldown.
