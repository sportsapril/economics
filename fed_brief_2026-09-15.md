## 🎙️ Sentiment Read

The read is hawkish, anchored to specifics rather than a vague tone score:

- **Dot plot direction:** The June SEP showed a median expectation of the fed funds rate reaching 3.8% by end-2026 (implying one hike this year). Futures markets are now pricing **two** quarter-point hikes by year-end (September and December). Deutsche Bank economists expect the updated dot plot to show at least one more hike this year, "with several officials projecting more than that."
- **Voting dynamics:** At the July meeting, the Fed held rates steady, but three regional presidents (Cleveland's Hammack, Minneapolis's Kashkari, Dallas's Logan) dissented in favor of an immediate hike. That hawkish minority appears to have grown into a majority view heading into September.
- **Warsh's own position is the key uncertainty.** He has talked tough on inflation (Jackson Hole) but has also emphasized underlying trends over isolated data points — and Aug core CPI's YoY tick-down (2.5%→2.4%) gives him a plausible, if narrow, justification to hold. As Freedom Capital Markets' Jay Woods put it, this "could be his one last line of defense" against a rate hike that markets and several colleagues now expect.
- **Political backdrop:** Trump administration officials have publicly pushed Warsh against hiking (reported by CNBC on Sept 5), an unusual dynamic given Warsh is a Trump appointee. This is commentary/reporting, not a Fed statement, but it's part of the context shaping how the decision will be read.
- **Inflation drivers cited by officials and reporters:** reinstituted tariffs, elevated energy/shipping costs, and supply-chain effects tied to the ongoing Iran conflict are cited as reasons goods inflation has reaccelerated even as the labor market holds up (August unemployment steady at 4.1%).
