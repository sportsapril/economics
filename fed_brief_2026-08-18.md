The July 29 statement language was "nearly identical" to the prior meeting's, per Advisor Perspectives' recap, reiterating that "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain sectors, including energy," and committing to "deliver price stability." The Committee held rates 9–3, with three regional presidents — Beth Hammack (Cleveland), Neel Kashkari (Minneapolis), and Lorie Logan (Dallas) — dissenting in favor of a rate *increase*, citing inflation that has run above the 2% target for more than five years.

Those hike odds have since cooled — from ~63% in late July to ~32% as of Aug 17 — as the July jobs report showed an outright payrolls decline. The net picture is a Fed leadership (now under new Chair Kevin Warsh) still leaning hawkish on above-target inflation, but data-dependent enough that a weak jobs print visibly moved the odds toward a hold. Tomorrow's July FOMC minutes should show how divided the Committee actually was in the room.
