## 🎙️ Sentiment Read
With no new Fed communications in the last 24 hours, sentiment is being driven by the data-versus-dot-plot tension rather than fresh Fed rhetoric. The June dot plot (median 3.8% year-end) and the 9-3 vote split at the July meeting — a historically rare three-way hawkish dissent — show a committee that leaned toward "we may need to hike" as of late July. Chair Warsh has deliberately withheld forward guidance, which several commentators (e.g., Pantheon Macroeconomics' Samuel Tombs, cited by Benzinga) say increases meeting-to-meeting uncertainty. Tombs said the recent data mean "Mr. Warsh is very unlikely to sound stridently hawkish" at the next meeting, and Pantheon now expects the policy rate to stay unchanged for the rest of the year.

The specific wording to watch for is not yet available — the July FOMC statement's language and Wednesday's minutes will be the next hard evidence of how the committee's internal debate is evolving. Until then, this is a market interpreting incoming inflation and labor data as walking back a hawkish tilt that the Fed itself has not yet confirmed or denied.
