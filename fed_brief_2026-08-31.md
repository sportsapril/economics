## 🏛️ What's New From the Fed

**Chairman Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote (Friday, August 28)** — the dominant Fed event of the window. Speaking at the Kansas City Fed's Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium, Warsh struck a notably hawkish tone in his first keynote as chairman:

- On inflation: "We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed. Otherwise, we have work to do." He said he was "impressed" with the economy's overall strength but flagged that "underlying trends" in inflation have not improved.
- On growth/AI: "The potential for substantially higher growth is on the rise. Ever-expanding pools of capital are pouring into AI-related infrastructure of all sorts."
- On forward guidance: He pushed back on markets over-anchoring to Fed signals, saying the Fed "should not indulge a regime in which market participants are looking primarily to the Fed for their next trade."
- He characterized the labor market as stable and near full employment while describing growth as strengthening.

## 🎙️ Sentiment Read

**Hawkish, and more specific than expected.** The market's own read is the clearest signal here: the roughly 23-point jump in hike odds (35% → 58%) in a single day is a large repricing for a Fed communications event, and Deutsche Bank was quoted saying it was "surprised" by the "specificity and hawkish direction" of the speech — the bank continues to expect two 25bp hikes this year (September and December). Warsh's own language — "otherwise, we have work to do" — is a direct signal that further tightening is on the table if inflation trends don't improve, a notably firmer construction than typical Fed hedging language.

Context matters: this was Warsh's third major public appearance as chairman, and according to multiple accounts his June remarks were similarly hawkish, July's were read as more dovish (raising doubts he'd follow through), and Friday's "split the difference" — hawkish enough to move markets, but with the equity reaction more contained than after June. That pattern of oscillation is itself a data point: markets remain genuinely uncertain whether Warsh is signaling a firm intention to hike or preserving optionality.
