## 🎙️ Sentiment Read

The Fed's internal signal is genuinely split, which is the story right now more than any single hawkish or dovish tilt:

- **Hawkish evidence:** Three FOMC dissents in July favoring an immediate hike — an event without recent precedent — plus Chair Warsh's own rhetoric emphasizing that inflation has now run above the 2% target for over five years running (cited as "64 consecutive months" in commentary) and that price stability, not employment, is his primary concern right now.
- **Dovish counter-evidence:** The July jobs report (payrolls -23,000, weakest in years) has visibly cooled hike odds at CME FedWatch, cutting the implied hike probability roughly in half within a week.
- **Net read:** This is not a "quiet, no-change" environment. It's an FOMC and a market both genuinely uncertain whether the next move is a hike or a hold — commentary (CNN, Motley Fool) explicitly frames Warsh and colleagues as being at a "crossroads." Today's July CPI print is likely to be the single most important near-term data point in resolving that uncertainty.
