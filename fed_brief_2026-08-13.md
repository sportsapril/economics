## 🎙️ Sentiment Read

The policy conversation has whipsawed in the last two weeks in a way that leaves the Fed's own July 29 statement looking dated. At that meeting, three regional presidents dissented in favor of a **hike**, and the statement described a labor market with job gains "keeping pace with the workforce." Eight days later (8/7), the July jobs report showed nonfarm payrolls **fell by 23,000** (vs. +83,000 expected), with May and June revised down a combined 103,000, and average hourly earnings growth slowing to 3.2% y/y — the weakest since May 2021. That single data point flipped CME FedWatch pricing from majority-hike to majority-cut within days.

New Chair Kevin Warsh (sworn in 5/22/2026, succeeding Jerome Powell) has been characterized in unnamed-source reporting (Financial Times, cited by 24/7 Wall St. and others) as open to a September hike *if* inflation surprised to the upside in the August 12 CPI report. That report came in **in line with, and a touch cooler than,** consensus, which — layered on top of the jobs shock — removed the immediate case for that scenario and reinforced market pricing toward a cut. This is sourced to unnamed-source reporting about Warsh's thinking, not a direct statement from him, and should be read with that caveat. ([247wallst.com](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/07/the-aug-12-inflation-report-could-decide-whether-kevin-warsh-raises-rates-in-september/))

Net: the specific, verifiable signal (FedWatch pricing, the CPI print, the jobs data) points dovish for September; the specific verifiable signal from the Fed's own last meeting (the statement language and the hawkish 3-way dissent) points hawkish and predates the data shift. The FOMC minutes on 8/19 are the next opportunity to see how the Committee itself is reconciling that gap.
